Life in the tide pools

By Ana Ruiz

Tide pools are small worlds that the sea leaves behind twice a day. Anemones, crabs and small fish wait in them for the water to come back, and each pool keeps its own balance of light, salt and warmth while the tide is out.

Visit at low tide and walk slowly. The rocks are slippery, and the animals you are looking for hide under ledges and in the weed. A field guide helps you put names to what you see.

Leave everything where you found it. Turning a stone over is fine if you turn it back, gently, the way it was, so whatever lives under it still has a roof.

When the water comes back the pools join the sea again, and by the next low tide everything has moved a little. No two visits are the same, which is most of the fun.
